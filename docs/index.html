Why radar can hear salt

Salt dissolved in soil water adds ionic conductivity, the imaginary part of permittivity, ε″. It changes the phase between HH and VV echoes far more than water does, so phase separates salt from moisture where amplitude cannot.

Two modes

  • Global (any dual-pol scene, HH+HV): flags land that is wet yet bare all season, typical for saline groundwater and failing crops.
  • Precision (quad-pol): the HH–VV phase, the direct dielectric fingerprint of salt.

Honest limits

  • Provisional NISAR products. Each scene has a constant phase offset (+61° Jun/Jul, −96° Oct), so phase is measured relative to dry Thar sand (ε″ ≈ 0).
  • Surface roughness also nudges phase; the salt flat is smoother than dunes. Not yet controlled.
  • The risk layer is a screening tool; field ECe samples are needed to calibrate it.
  • L-band sees salt weakly. Our long-term goal: a P-band receiver CubeSat (SALOPI).
