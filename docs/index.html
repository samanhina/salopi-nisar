Forward model. SALOPI soil dielectric model at 1.257 GHz (Stogryn saline water + Dobson mixing) with first-order Bragg (SPM) backscatter. Roughness cancels in the SPM co-pol phase. Over 20 000 random soils, log-salinity explains 74% of HH–VV phase variance, moisture 13%, incidence angle 6%.

Natural experiment on NISAR quad-pol data. Great Rann of Kutch salt flat vs non-saline Thar dunes; zones defined geographically; bare-surface pixels only (HV−HH < −5 dB, above noise).

Physical basis

Dissolved salt adds ionic conductivity σ to soil water, raising the imaginary permittivity ε″ = σ/(2πfε₀). A complex ε rotates the phase between HH and VV backscatter; water mainly raises ε′, which changes amplitude but hardly the phase. Phase therefore separates salt from moisture where amplitude cannot.

Processing

  • NISAR L2 GCOV (20 m), 10×10 multilook → 200 m. Complex HH·VV* averaged before taking the angle.
  • Phase referenced to dry Thar dunes (ε″ ≈ 0) to remove the per-scene calibration offset of provisional products (+61° Jun/Jul, −96° Oct).
  • Risk = P(soil moisture) × (1 − P(season-max HV)), P = percentile within scene; open water removed.
  • Salt signature = bare surface with phase ≥ +5° on every available date (≥ 2 dates).

Limitations

  • Provisional products; one quad-pol site.
  • Roughness differences (smooth crust vs dunes) not yet separated.
  • Risk is a screening index; calibration needs field ECe samples.
  • L-band sensitivity to salt is weak; the SALOPI concept targets P-band (250–380 MHz) signals of opportunity.
