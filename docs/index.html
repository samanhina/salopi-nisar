How it works

Why radar can see salt

Salt dissolved in soil water adds an ionic conductivity term to the soil's permittivity (the imaginary part, ε″). Our physics model (Stogryn saline water + Dobson soil mixing + Fresnel reflection) shows the effect grows at lower frequencies: at L-band (NISAR, 1.25 GHz) salt changes the echo by ~0.4 dB, while moisture changes it by ~2 dB — so amplitude alone confuses salt with water.

The phase difference between HH and VV reacts to ε″ much more than to moisture. In our 240-case virtual experiment, phase carried 55% of the salt signal, amplitude carried 60% of the moisture signal.

Two modes
  • Global mode (any NISAR dual-pol scene, HH+HV): flags land that is wet (NISAR SME2 soil moisture) yet stays bare all season (low HV). Shallow saline groundwater and failing crops look exactly like this.
  • Precision mode (quad-pol scenes): adds the HH–VV phase — the direct dielectric fingerprint of salt.
Evidence: is it really salt?

1 · Physics. Our SALOPI model at NISAR's 1.26 GHz (Stogryn + Dobson + Bragg surface scattering): a salt crust shifts HH–VV phase by +4…+9°; moisture alone by only −0.2°. Salt explains 74% of phase variance, moisture 13%.

2 · Real NISAR data, natural experiment. Great Rann of Kutch salt flat vs non-saline Thar sand dunes (zones set by geography, bare pixels only):

  • The salt flat sits +9°, +14°, +5° above sand on all three dates — same sign and size as the model.
  • It is not moisture: NISAR's own soil-moisture product says the salt flat is drier than the dunes.
  • Control (Khadir/Bela islands) flips sign between dates — no stable shift.
  • Phase alone tells salt flat from sand with 77–90% accuracy (AUC 0.75–0.95).
Honest limits
  • These are provisional NISAR products. Each scene carries a constant HH–VV phase offset (+61° in June/July, −96° in October) from calibration still in progress, so we measure phase relative to dry Thar sand dunes, a natural target with ε″ ≈ 0.
  • Roughness and surface type also change the phase a little; the salt flat is smoother than the dunes. We control for vegetation (bare pixels only) and moisture, but not yet for roughness.
  • The risk layer is a screening indicator, not a salinity measurement. It needs field checks (ECe samples) to calibrate.
  • At L-band the salt effect is weak; our long-term goal is a P-band receiver (SALOPI CubeSat) that listens to existing satellite signals.
